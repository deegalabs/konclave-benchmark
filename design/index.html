1 Summary

The third deliverable of Milestone 1 (ZCG grant #486) asks for "the written design for new and existing vaults, with a verdict on viability, including whether an in-place upgrade of an existing vault is possible and the case where phones are not enough"[1].

Verdict

A phone of the A72's class can prove a 2-action payment in the browser within the bar (R1). With the scan we measured, none of the three phones kept the vault's books within the bars: the A72 failed the one-day bar and was over the first-scan bar on every run (R3 and R4, Table 3). The A72's PASS label for the proof rests on a temperature rule we chose after the runs. Whichever way its runs are counted, each of the five took less than half the bar (Table 3, note a).

Keeping the books means scanning the chain for the vault and holding its balance and history. Signing was not measured. The members' devices sign today, on phones included.

For new vaults, every member's device would derive the viewing key from a secret the members agree on while the vault is created, and the key would never reach the coordinator. A phone would still run Konclave: it would hold its share and the viewing key, check a payment, approve it and sign, and a phone of the A72's class can prove (R1). What a phone would not do by default is keep the books. One member's computer would keep them, build each payment and prove it, and phones would receive a summary of the vault's state, sealed to them and signed by that member's seat (section 5).

The application promised an alternative if phones were not enough, and wrote that promise for the proof. We measured the opposite split: the proof fits and the scan does not. So the alternative is for scanning: the member's computer above. A vault with no computer is allowed, with limits: it would scan on a phone while the app is open, which is the slow case (section 5.8), and it is the first work to move out of Milestone 2 if the hours do not cover it (section 10.1).

For existing vaults the answer is: in part. An existing vault's viewing key was drawn at random and cannot be derived again, so an upgrade can only move a copy that exists. A protected vault could move its key to its members' devices in place, with no new key and no move of funds. It depends on two things that are open: the Milestone 3 items of Table 11 have to close, and a vault created before v0.18.0 first needs a certificate step that is not designed. The vaults in use today were likely created before v0.18.0. An open vault has no in-place path (section 8).

The other members would trust one member for the books, though not for who is paid: a payment still needs the quorum's signatures, and each device checks it against the approved payment. That member would choose the notes and the fee within what a device accepts (Table 7). What the coordinator can still do to a payment is in section 2. The check on who is paid rests on two rules that are not written yet (section 5.5). Every member's device and every backup a member exports would hold the viewing key and the secret it is derived from. With one computer in the vault, a payment waits for that computer.

This is a privacy change. It does not make a vault independent of the coordinator. The coordinator would still keep the proposals and the votes, could still stop every payment by not answering, and until issue #567 is fixed would still supply the approved payment that every device compares against (section 2).

Three hour lines of Milestone 2 would change. Two would read "on the device of the member who keeps the books" where the application says "on the device". The first would no longer have one member's device create the viewing key and seal it to the others: each device would derive it. The change adds work that no filed line sizes. Whether the 140 filed hours cover it is open. Section 10.1 proposes what Milestone 2 delivers in any case, what moves out first if they do not, and that the amount stays as filed. We ask ZCG to accept or discuss this before Milestone 2 starts.

The sk decision. ZIP 2005 says the members of a FROST group must agree on a secret value, sk, and derive from it the parts of the viewing key that the key generation does not give. No vault has one today. Our decision of 24 November 2026 is that new vaults adopt ZIP 2005 in Milestone 2. It changes the filed text of Milestone 2, so we ask ZCG to accept or discuss it (section 10). How the members agree on sk is open, and our proposal is in section 9.1. Existing vaults keep their keys and get no sk.

Status. Nothing in this design is built, and nothing in it has been accepted by ZCG. It is not a security audit. Of the five deliverables of Milestone 1, the benchmark is delivered with no outside run yet, the WebZjs evaluation is delivered, this design is delivered as a draft, the upstream reports are delivered in part, and the independent review has not started, because ZCG has not named a reviewer (appendix B). The verdict above is ours until that review. Later changes of status are posted in the grant's forum topic.

2 What this design does not change

The design takes one thing off the coordinator, the viewing key. Seven things stay as they are today.

  1. The approved payment (issue #567). Today each device compares the transaction it is asked to sign with the approved payment, and the approved payment comes from the coordinator at signing time. A proposal's signed write binds the proposer's name, not what the proposal pays. So a coordinator that changed a proposal after it was approved could serve the changed one to every device that compares, and the comparison would match. Until issue #567 is fixed, a payment the quorum signs is only as safe as the coordinator. In the new mode the books computer would also read the approved proposal from the coordinator, the only place proposals are kept, so taking the build off the coordinator does not narrow this. The fix is ADR-0012 steps 4a and 4b: each approval bound to the content of its proposal and checked on the device. The books computer would need the same check on the proposal it builds from. The count of the other seats' votes that a device shows is also the coordinator's word until step 4b. Issue #567 is open and scheduled.
  2. Names and proposals (issue #601). The coordinator stores the members' names and every proposal in plain text. A proposal names who is paid, how much, and the memo. So the coordinator would still read every payment the vault proposes, as the application states[1]. Issue #601 is open and not scheduled. Its plan has two parts: the names can be sealed now, and the proposals only once the devices build the transactions, which is what Milestone 2 does for new vaults.
  3. The light client server and the proxy. The server the books computer scans from and sends through, and the gRPC-web proxy in front of it, would see that device's IP address and access times, and each transaction it sends (Table 7). Inference They could learn which transactions belong to the vault if the queries are not batched with decoys. If we run the proxy, the same operator as the coordinator sees this. A server that accepts gRPC-web directly, such as zec.global, run by Zingo Labs, removes the proxy and with it the second party. That server still sees the same: the device's IP address, its access times and the transactions it sends. A page on another origin reached zec.global directly in our runs (R23, section 11.5). Issue #516 records the memo fetch that hands the server which transactions are the vault's, with batching into block windows with decoys as the known mitigation, and the application cites it for this exposure. No plan yet, beyond deciding who runs the proxy (Table 11).
  4. The web host (issue #605). A key kept on a device is only as safe as the app the web host serves to it. A modified app served to every member at once is not stopped by the threshold. Issue #605 is open and scheduled. Its plan: a published list of the hash of every file of each release, a script anyone can run to compare the live site with it, and an installed app that refuses an update that is not a published release. The issue says the refusal blocks an honest mistake and not an attack: against a modified app it gives a warning, shown only to a member whose app is open when the update arrives.
  5. The coordinator offline (issues #613 and #614). Proposals, votes and the approved payment all come from the coordinator, so while it is offline or refuses to answer, no payment can be made. The application states this limit[1]. hanh, of the ZCG committee, raised it on the forum: "the coordinator can be offline and lock the funds" (topic 57218, post 14). Both issues are open and not scheduled. Issue #613 is a path for the members to spend from their backups with none of our servers running. Issue #614 is research on proposals and votes that do not depend on one server.
  6. The relay. The grant does not reduce what the relay sees today (Table 7), and the new mode adds to it: the sealed summaries' size and timing, and which device posts them. Issue #340, on what the signing room shows the relay, is open with no decision. No plan yet for the rest.
  7. The past of a vault that moves its key. The coordinator has held that key, and the key does not change (section 8.3).

3 The coordinator today

Konclave's hosted coordinator holds the viewing key of every vault. It never receives, derives or stores a share and cannot move funds on its own, but it builds the transactions the quorum signs, and until issue #567 is fixed a payment the quorum signs is only as safe as the coordinator (section 2)[2].

With the key, the coordinator scans the chain for the vault, keeps its balance and history, checks a new proposal against the balance, builds each transaction and makes its proof. The members' devices hold their shares, check the transaction they are handed, and sign it. A member's device also assembles the signing package and aggregates the signature. The browser does not scan[2][3].

The viewing key lets the coordinator read every vault's balances, payments, amounts and memos, payslips included[4]. The grant pays to take that key off the coordinator. What the coordinator would still read afterwards is in section 2.

If the key leaves the coordinator, a member's device has to scan, keep the wallet between sessions, choose the notes a payment spends, build the transaction and prove it. Native wallets on phones do all of this. Konclave runs in a browser, and two things make a phone's browser the hard case:

  • A page gets threads in WebAssembly only when it is cross-origin isolated. That needs two security headers that Konclave's app does not send[1]. The work runs on one thread unless the app changes.
  • A browser can suspend a hidden tab, and its storage can be cleared.

Since NU6.3 no value enters the Orchard pool (ZIP 2006, a consensus rule), and a wallet must not pay an external receiver there (ZIP 326, a draft for wallets). A payment to an Orchard-protocol address now lands in the Ironwood pool. The Sapling pool still takes funds, and Konclave does not use it. So the measurement had to use the current crates and include Ironwood.

4 What we measured

4.1 Method

We timed proofs and scans in a browser with our own harness, konclave-benchmark (MIT OR Apache-2.0)[5]. It follows the method of ChainSafe's zcash-wasm-benchmark, which has no licence, so none of its code is in ours[6]. It uses crates from the zcash organisation, among them orchard 0.16.0, and proves with the Action circuit that crate uses since NU6.3 (OrchardCircuitVersion::PostNu6_3), which is the same for the Orchard and Ironwood pools.

A proof row covers the key build, the preparation and the first proof, for 2 actions, with threads off. A scan row covers the download, decoding, trial decryption and the note commitment trees, across the Orchard and Ironwood pools. Every scan uses one published test key, so anyone can repeat it. The harness has no wallet database, no Sapling and no storage.

A harness time is not a sync time. We did not show that it is a lower bound for a wallet either. No run put the harness and a wallet on one machine and one range. The one pair we have is not like for like. The WebZjs wallet, with threads on, on our desktop, synced 55,168 blocks in 211 s (run-3530869.json). The harness, on one thread, took 388.78 s for 50,000 blocks on the Dell (R19). The machines, the threads and the ranges differ, and the wallet takes subtree roots from the server (the finished top hashes of parts of the note commitment tree, which a server can hand over so that a device does not rebuild them) and builds subtrees in parallel, while the harness hashes every leaf on one thread. On the A72 the same wallet build synced 70,446 blocks in 956.0 s (S5), against the harness's 897.86 s for 50,000 blocks in Chrome (R14), with the same differences except the machine. So a wallet can be faster or slower than the harness, and the verdict below is about the scan we measured.

4.3 Where the time goes

On R3 the download took 0.60 s, decryption 57.39 s and the tree 68.20 s. Decryption alone is nearly twice the bar. Per action, decryption cost 0.98 ms on the A72 (R3), 0.31 ms on the S24 (R9) and 0.48 ms on the Dell (R18), and the tree 1.16, 0.34 and 0.52 ms. On every phone the tree took slightly more than half of the two stages (52 to 55%). The A14 is about three times slower per action than the A72, and the A72 about three times slower than the S24.

The one-day range is a busy day. It holds 58,622 actions, 16.96 per block, against 7.35 in the first-scan range[9]. Of them, 207 are in the Orchard pool and 58,415 in the Ironwood pool. 153 of its 3,456 blocks, all between 3,579,935 and 3,580,346, hold 46,274 of its actions (78.9%; daily-perblock-actions.json). Phantom (Udoka-AM) published this burst on the forum on 18 November 2026, the day after the verdict runs, and our own count confirms it[10]. The range was fixed by the published rule before anyone counted its actions, and the bar is on that range.

The burst does not explain the A72's result. A rehearsal on 12 November, with the same harness WebAssembly as the verdict build, scanned a 3,456-block range with no burst (3,500,000 to 3,503,455, 22,212 actions) in 60.37 to 61.47 s on the A72 in Firefox 159 (session 927d5d58, appendix A.4). It is not a result, and its range precedes NU7. It is still twice the bar. We did not run the S24 on a range with no burst.

Read A scanner that skips large transactions would lose the vault's own notes, because a payroll is itself a large transaction. The harness has a test for it (a_note_in_a_big_transaction_is_found_by_default_and_lost_with_a_filter, tests/scan.rs:133). The cost of scanning grows with the number of actions on chain, so a busy day is the case to plan for[11].

4.4 Threads, a parallel tree and a native scan

The rows of this section and of sections 4.5 and 4.6 are information rows. They ran on 20, 22 and 23 November, after the verdict session, most of them in Chrome on another build (5ac328d), by methods fixed in our own notes and not posted. They change no label of Table 3. Every condition of every run is in the addendum README[12].

With 4 threads on the A72 in Chrome, decryption fell from 55.91 s to 23.16 s, 2.4 times faster, and the tree stage stayed at 66.93 s, because the harness filled the tree on one thread (R25). The one-day range took 92.37 s. A 2-action proof with 4 threads took 54.37 s (R24), against 88.72 s on one thread in the same browser (R11).

Read zcash_client_backend 0.25 builds subtrees in parallel when it saves scanned blocks (data_api/ll/wallet.rs:1582), and the store WebZjs pins does the same (zcash_client_memory b4c8f0d, src/wallet_write.rs:563). We added a harness mode, subtrees, that builds pieces of 1,024 leaves in parallel and inserts them in order, as that code does. It is our code following the library, not the library's own code path. Measured At 4 threads it lowered the tree stage by 23% on the A72 (69.11 to 52.94 s) and by 31% on the S24 (32.12 to 22.12 s), both replayed from a saved copy of the range (sessions 3a9d24c9 and 75289f16 on the A72, c1ac4c4b and d3e076f3 on the S24, in the addendum README). With one thread it is slower than the default.

The best one-day rows with threads and subtrees together, against the 30 s bar, are below. "Replayed" reads the range from a saved copy with no network, and "live" downloads it.

  • A72, 4 threads: 76.12 s replayed (R42) and 119.02 s live (R45). Over the bar.
  • S24, 4 threads (R46, R49): 30.13 s replayed (runs 29.37 to 32.96 s, two of the five under 30 s) and 31.07 s live (runs 30.80 to 31.66 s, all five over 30 s). The bars are judged on live rows, so the S24 was over.
  • Dell, 12 threads: 24.98 s (R29). With one thread and the default tree the same laptop took 58.14 s (R26). The laptop has no scan bar.

No phone was under 30 s without threads. In the same Chrome sessions the one-thread totals were 142.57 s on the A72 (R42) and 70.29 s on the S24 (R46). Taking the tree stage out leaves about 56 s on the A72 (142.57 less 86.13) and about 29 s on the S24 (70.29 less 41.02). So on the A72 the scan cannot pass on one thread whatever the tree does, and both stages have to fall.

On the desktop the one-day range took 11.34 s natively on one thread with the default tree, against 48.15 s in headless Chromium on the same machine (R38, R30). With 16 threads and subtrees it took 4.08 s natively against 18.12 s (R41, R33). These rows ran under WSL2 on a desktop. We did not run a native scan on a phone, so we do not know what WebAssembly costs there.

4.8 What the results mean for the design

Each point below is an inference from one unit per model (section 12).

  1. A member's phone can prove a 2-action payment if it proves at least as fast as this A72, with the page visible. A phone of the A14's class does not: every run was over the bar.
  2. With the scan we measured, a phone of the A72's or the A14's class does not keep the books within the bars. Both were over every scan bar on every run. The S24 was under the first-scan bar on every run and over the one-day bar.
  3. Threads and a parallel tree, together, did not bring the A72 under 30 s. They brought the S24 to the bar and not under it on live rows.
  4. What we did not measure could change point 2: a wallet whose store takes subtree roots from the server (section 5.6), and a native scan on a phone, which Milestone 2 would time first (section 10.1).
  5. Storage needs the per-block records kept in check before a device can hold a year of them.
  6. Our laptop scanned the busy day in about a minute on one thread (R18, R26), and in 24.98 s replayed from a saved copy with 12 threads and a parallel tree (R29). That is the basis of the default in section 5.

5.3 Creating a vault

  1. The devices create the vault as they do today, through the relay, and every member confirms the creation code.
  2. Undecided how. Each device would draw a random value, its part of sk, during the key generation. This is our proposal for how the members agree on sk. The alternative, in which one member draws sk alone, is in section 9.1.
  3. Undecided how. Each device would seal its part to every other device, to the keys the devices exchange when the vault is created, as the second-round packages of the key generation are sealed today, and never to a key taken from the coordinator's answer. The sealing would have a post-quantum part, which Konclave's sealing does not have today (section 9.1).
  4. The relay would carry the sealed parts. No request to the coordinator would carry one.
  5. Each device would compute sk from all the parts, taken in the order of the seats, and derive nk and rivk_ext from it with use_qsk = true (section 9.1). The key's ak is the vault's group key, in the even-y form the draft text of ZIP 312 requires. So every device would build the viewing key itself, and no device would send it to another.
  6. Undecided. That every device derived the same sk. The sealing we would reuse does not say who sealed, so anyone who knows the key a device is sealed to, the relay's operator included, could seal it a part of their own in place of a member's. That device would then derive another sk and another viewing key. The method in the draft text of ZIP 312 catches this with a commitment to sk in what every participant signs in its last round. The proposed equivalent is a commitment to sk in the creation record every seat signs (step 8). Each device would check the other seats' records against its own commitment, as it checks the read-key hash today (ADR-0012 D4), and would show the address only when none differs. A vault opens when enough seats agree, so a device that is not among them has to make that check itself.
  7. The members would compare the key's code before the address is shown. Until step 6 is settled this is the only check that every device derived the same nk and rivk_ext, and the draft text of ZIP 312 requires every participant to end with the same values of ak, nk and rivk_ext. What the code is computed from, how long it is, and whether someone who can choose sk, or the last part of it, could search for one with a matching code are not specified (Table 11).
  8. Undecided. The vault's mode and a commitment to sk in the record every seat signs. That needs a new creation protocol and an amendment to ADR-0012. A device would read the mode from its own record and would not take it from the coordinator's answer. The seats would sign that record only after each has derived sk, later than today (ADR-0012 D4). The commitment must not reveal sk, and sk itself would be in no record.
  9. The coordinator would register the vault without the key and without sk, from the first registration that counts, which any device holding the creation bundle may post, as today (ADR-0012 D5). Its registration code would have no field for a viewing key or for sk, and a test would fail if one were added. A second test, in the app, would fail if any request body to the coordinator contained the viewing key or its parts nk and rivk_ext, sk, qsk or any device's part of sk, in any encoding. Milestone 2's acceptance criteria ask for both for the viewing key: no request to the coordinator carries it, and a test on the registration code[1]. We would add sk, qsk and the parts of sk to both.

The order of steps 7 to 9 is not fixed. No step exists for the height the scan starts from: who records it, and how every device comes to hold the same one, is gap 1 (Table 12), and it gates the first Milestone 2 line.

5.4 Keeping the books, and the summary

The books computer would scan the chain for the vault, keep the wallet in an encrypted store (section 5.6) and publish a summary of the vault's state to the other devices.

  1. The books computer would ask a light client server for the chain's new blocks, and for the memos of the vault's own transactions. A browser reaches that server through a gRPC-web proxy, unless the server answers a browser directly (section 2). Who runs the proxy is open (Table 11).
  2. The server sends the blocks. The computer has to take its word for them: the server can leave outputs out, delay them or make them up (Table 7).
  3. The computer would try to decrypt every output with the vault's viewing key. The ones that open are the vault's. It would add them to its wallet, which it keeps encrypted (section 5.6).
  4. It would write a summary of what the wallet now shows, and sign it with its seat's write key, so that a phone can tell which seat made it.
  5. It would seal the summary to the other members' devices and post it to the relay. The relay carries it and holds no key that opens it.
  6. Undecided. Where the summary waits until a phone asks for it. Today's relay cannot hold it: it keeps a room for one hour after its last use and evicts rooms under pressure[19]. The options are listed below.
  7. A phone would fetch the summary when its member opens the app. It may arrive late, or be an older one.
  8. The phone would check the signature against the write key of the seat that keeps the books, and refuse a summary that does not verify.
  9. Undecided. How a phone learns that the summary is the latest, and what it shows when it is not. A rule is proposed below.

The rule for the summary.

  • Content. What a device needs to show: balance, notes and history lines. The wallet itself is too large: the relay accepts posts of up to 128 KiB in rooms of up to 4 MiB[19], and the wallet is projected to grow by tens of megabytes a year (S1). The format and the size are open.
  • Signing key. The write key of the books computer's seat, the Ed25519 key that seat already uses for its votes, renames, proposals and sends (ADR-0011). The seat's share would not sign it: the share signs nothing new beyond its certificate (ADR-0012 D8).
  • A domain of its own. The signed message would carry a domain of its own, added to the closed list in konclave-seal in which no domain is a prefix of another, among them the write message (konclave-write-v1), the vote envelope reserved for issue #567, and the certificate's (ADR-0012 D2). Then a write signature the coordinator holds could not pass as a summary on a phone, and a summary signature could not verify as a vote or a send. This extends what the write key signs and needs an amendment to ADR-0011.
  • Which seat. A device would accept a summary only from the seat the vault names as its books computer, named in something the seats sign and never by the coordinator's answer. Every seat's certificate is in a device's bundle, so the signature check alone passes for any seat. Until the vault names the seat, any member could sign a summary that a phone accepts. How the vault names it is open.
  • Verification. A device would check the signature against the write key in that seat's certificate, read from the bundle the device recorded at the vault's creation (ADR-0012 D1 and D4), and would refuse a summary that does not verify. A device restored from a backup has no bundle, so it needs the certificates kept and served first (ADR-0012 step 4b). That part is open.
  • One implementation. The signed message and its check would live once, in konclave-seal, as the certificate's do.
  • Freshness. The signature says which seat made a summary, not that it is the latest. The relay could hold one back or deliver an older one again. For completeness and freshness, the trust is in the member who keeps the books, the relay and the light client server (Table 11). The rule proposed below narrows part of that and removes none of the three. If the summary waits in a blob the coordinator keeps, the coordinator joins that list. We propose this rule, and it is not settled:
    • Each summary would carry, inside what is signed, a counter that only rises and the block height it covers.
    • A phone would order summaries by the covered height first and the counter second. It would keep the highest it has accepted from the seat the vault names, and accept only a higher one, or the same one with the same signed bytes. An older summary could then not replace a newer one on that phone, and two different summaries at one height and counter would show as a conflict.
    • A phone would not take as its latest a summary whose covered height is above the tip it reads. A seat then cannot push the order past the chain and shut out the summaries after it, and a books computer restored from a backup does not start below what the phones hold.
    • The order is kept per seat, so a seat that takes the role starts its own. What a phone does with the summaries of the seat that held the role before is open, with how the role moves (section 5.9).
    • A phone would read the height of the chain's tip from a light client server, which needs no scan, and show "updated N blocks ago".
    • The books computer would publish on a schedule, so that a growing age tells the member something.
    What the rule costs and does not give:
    • It does not reveal a summary that is held back. The phone only sees the age grow.
    • The covered height is the word of the member who keeps the books. A dishonest one can sign the tip's height over an old state, and the age then looks current. The age tells a member that delivery stalled. It does not tell that the state is true.
    • It does not show that two phones were sent different summaries. Each summary is sealed to its own device and each verifies. Only a comparison between devices, or a scan, shows that.
    • The tip height is the light client server's word, and asking for it shows that server the phone's IP address and access times. A server that reports a lower tip makes an old summary look current. So the phone would read the tip through a path the relay's operator does not run, where it can, and would not let the tip fall below the highest it has seen.
    • A new or restored phone, or a browser whose storage was cleared, has nothing to compare with. It still computes the age from the covered height, so an old summary delivered to it again shows as old, unless the member who keeps the books signed a false height.
    The other option is a store the phone can ask, with the cost of that store (next item).
  • Where it waits. Three options, none chosen. The maintainer settles it, and ZCG if a server DeegaLabs runs, the coordinator or the relay, keeps the summary.
    • The coordinator keeps one sealed blob per vault, behind the read key, and holds no key that opens it. Cost: this gives the coordinator a role the application's Side Effects text says it loses ("removing from the coordinator any copy of that information"), so it would go to ZCG and need its own ADR. The coordinator could hold a summary back or serve an older one, and would see its size and timing. It would also keep, at rest and in its backups, a copy of the books sealed with today's sealing, so a party that later obtains that storage and has a quantum computer could read them (section 9.1), and it would see when each device fetches the summary.
    • A relay that keeps summaries. Cost: today's relay keeps nothing on disk[19], so this is a change to the relay, and it is not designed. The relay would see what it sees of a summary in any case (Table 7), and would also keep it at rest, with the same exposure to a later quantum computer as a blob on the coordinator. Who may write to that store is not designed. The relay is run by the same operator as the coordinator (section 5.2), so this option would also go to ZCG.
    • No store: the summary waits only in the relay's room. No party keeps a copy at rest, so this option gives no party more than Table 7 lists. Cost: with the relay as it is, the phone and the computer have to be open within one hour of each other. Otherwise the phone shows the summary it already holds, with its age.

Any member can verify the books. This is a principle of the design. Every member's device holds the viewing key, so any member can check the books by scanning on its own device, without asking another member. That scan still takes the word of the light client server it asks, as the books computer's does (Table 7). It starts from the height that gap 1 has to fix (Table 12), and it is independent only if the device reaches the server through a path the member who keeps the books does not run (Table 11). Neither is written yet. A device that does not scan cannot check the state it receives, because checking it is the scan itself, and a dishonest member keeping the books could do what Table 7 lists. The role of keeping the books follows from the measured limit and is no aim of the design: with the scan we measured, a phone did not keep the books within the bars (section 4.8). The role would narrow as a phone's scan gets faster, natively or by a cheaper method, and Milestone 2 would measure that first (section 10.1).

On a phone a full scan is the slow case. Two proposals make the check lighter, and both are optional (section 10.1).

  • A spot-check. A phone would scan a recent slice of the chain, for example the last day, with its own copy of the viewing key, and compare what it finds with the signed summary it holds. A mismatch would be shown to the member: the summary or one of the two light client servers is wrong, and the summary's signature says which seat made it. The check covers the history lines inside that slice only. It cannot check the balance or the notes, which depend on the blocks before the slice, so a line left out or made up before it is not caught. It checks the summary this phone holds, not the ones other phones were sent, and a phone that asks the same server as the books computer checks that member, not the server. On the A72 a one-day scan took 127.74 s (R3), and its trial decryption alone 57.39 s (section 4.3). Inference A check that spends nothing may not need the tree. Either way a member would start this check, and it would not run at every open.
  • Two computers. If two members' computers scan, their summaries could be compared and a disagreement shown to every member.

Choosing the device. Proposed for Milestone 2, and optional. Which device keeps the books is the group's choice and is never automatic. To inform it, each device would run a short scan test while the vault is created: a fixed set of blocks bundled with the app, scanned on the device with no network, so no server sees the test. The app would turn the result into one of three plain labels: keeps the books well, can keep them with limits, better not. The thresholds would be set from the posted bars, the 30 s one-day bar first (Table 2), and are not set yet. The app would suggest the device with the best label, and the group would confirm. The test could be run again at any time, for a new device or a phone that has slowed. A short test predicts roughly, since a phone slows as it heats (Table 17, L9). Each device's label would be shared sealed among the vault's devices only, and signed by its seat once the key generation has given it one, since the sealing does not say who sealed. A label is what that device reports, and a member could report any label, so the app would show it as reported, and the choice stays a choice of whom to trust with the books (Table 7). The test informs the choice. How the vault then names the chosen seat is an open item (Table 11).

5.5 A payment

  1. Today, 1. The coordinator keeps the proposals and the votes.
  2. Today, 2. With the viewing key it checks a proposal against the balance, builds the transaction and makes its proof.
  3. Today, 3 and 4. It seals the transaction to the signers, to the keys registered with it, once every member's device has registered its device key. The relay carries the sealed request[20].
  4. Today, 5. Each device recomputes the hash the signature covers (ZIP 244, as modified by ZIP 229 for version 6 transactions) from its own PCZT and refuses a signing package over any other message. It compares the transaction's outputs with the approved payment before it contributes a share[21].
  5. Today, 6. The device that aggregates posts the finished signatures to the signing room on the relay, and the coordinator reads them there.
  6. Today, 7. The coordinator sends the transaction to the network.
  7. Proposed, 1. The coordinator would keep the proposals and the votes, as today.
  8. Proposed, 2. The books computer would read the approved proposal from the coordinator. Until issue #567 is fixed it builds what the coordinator says was approved (section 2).
  9. Proposed, 3. The books computer would check the proposal against the balance, build and prove.
  10. Proposed, 4 and 5. The books computer would seal the transaction to the signers, to the keys in the seats' certificates, and the relay would carry it. The coordinator no longer has a transaction to seal. The envelope and the rule of whom it seals to exist once today, on the coordinator. Both would move to one place that the device and the coordinator link, and would not be written a second time. Open: whether a device accepts a signing request only from the seat the vault names as its books computer, signed with that seat's write key under a domain of its own, as for the summary (section 5.4).
  11. Proposed, 6. Each device would read the approved payment from the coordinator, as today.
  12. Proposed, 7. Every device would check and sign as in today's step 5. The approved payment a device compares against must never come from the builder's own signing request. If it did, the builder would write what its own transaction is checked against. That rule is not written yet. The same holds for the vault's own addresses: a device treats an output as change only if it pays an address the device derives from its own copy of the viewing key, never one named in the signing request or in a summary. Today a device takes those addresses from its own record, which was filled from the coordinator's first answer. In the new mode nothing yet says where they come from, so a books computer that named an address of its own as change could add an output to itself and take funds, and no device would question it. This is an open design item for Milestone 2. Our decision: the building line does not start before both rules are written (Table 11). The two rules guard against the builder. Against the coordinator only issue #567 does.
  13. Proposed, 8, undecided. How the finished signature reaches the books computer. Today it goes through the signing room on the relay (today, step 6). The design does not yet say whether the computer reads it there.
  14. Proposed, 9. The books computer would send the transaction through the light client server. From a browser that goes through the gRPC-web proxy (gap 3, Table 12).
  15. Proposed, 10, undecided. Who moves a proposal to sent, confirmed or superseded and holds the balance reservation, once the coordinator has no viewing key, and whether the coordinator is told the transaction id.

Not drawn, because it is open: what starts the build of an approved proposal on the books computer, and where a member gives the explicit confirmation that today is a signed send to the coordinator (ADR-0011). Every action that moves funds would keep a preview and an explicit confirmation, as today.

Two builders would exist side by side: the coordinator's for vaults in today's mode and the books computer's for the new mode. Both would be held to the one reading every device makes of a transaction before it signs. The books computer would choose which notes a payment spends and how many actions it has.

Each note a payment spends is signed under a key re-randomized for that spend with a value called alpha. Read Whoever builds the PCZT draws alpha, and each device signs under the alpha it reads from its own PCZT (konclave-wasm). Today the coordinator builds the PCZT, so it draws alpha. In the new mode the books computer would build it, so it would draw alpha. Who draws alpha matters because a party that draws it alone decides it alone. The draft text of ZIP 312 (zcash/zips#895) requires the randomizer to be derived from the signers' first-round commitments together with random bytes, and gives the reason: it hedges against a flawed random generator on the party that draws, and keeps that party from fully influencing the value. The pull request lists a builder-chosen one as its open issue 1. Konclave does not follow the draft here, as the Foundation's tools do not, and the new mode would not either until that issue is settled. Read Before the first round a device refuses a transaction in which a spend's alpha is zero or shared with another spend, or its re-randomized key is not the vault's group key under that alpha (konclave-seal, src/spends.rs). The new mode would keep that check. Whether a device should check more while the builder draws alpha is an open item (Table 11).

5.6 The wallet store, and making the scan faster

The books computer would keep the wallet in a store encrypted at rest, and the app would show the time of the last sync. Both are Milestone 2 lines[1]. What the store's key is derived from, and what a locked or reloaded app can show, are open (gap 2).

The per-block records take the test vault to a projected 86.8 MB in a year (S1). Milestone 2 has to keep them in check before a year fits under 50 MB. It could prune the records of blocks that hold none of the vault's notes, or use another means. We have not measured either.

Four things could make the scan faster. The default does not depend on any of them, and none is counted in the verdict.

  • Threads and a parallel tree. Measured (section 4.4). Threads need the whole page cross-origin isolated. In our rows the page was served isolated by the benchmark's own server, since the service worker did not isolate the page in Chrome on the phone. What isolation would break in Konclave's app is not listed yet (gap 4). Without it no phone was under 30 s, so the phones-only case needs this gap closed. With threads, no phone was under 30 s on a live row either (section 4.4).
  • Subtree roots from the server. Not measured. The device would take the roots of finished subtrees from the light client server and build only the parts of the tree that hold the vault's notes, and would not hash every leaf. Read Light client servers serve GetSubtreeRoots, zcash_client_backend uses them and WebZjs's store keeps them. Inference It would cut most of the tree's time. Read The roots are not authenticated: zcash_client_backend 0.25 stores them as served (src/sync.rs:229 to 299). Inference A wrong root cannot add or hide a note, because notes come from trial decryption. It gives the wallet a wrong anchor (the tree root a spend refers to) and wrong witnesses, so a spend built on it fails and the payment does not go out.
  • Checkpoints only for recent blocks. Not measured. The harness keeps a tree checkpoint for every block (appendix A.1). A wallet needs them only for the last blocks that a chain reorganisation could undo.
  • A native scan. Measured on a desktop only (section 4.4). Konclave's desktop shell could scan natively. We did not price it.

5.7 The scanner: three paths

Milestone 2 chooses the scanner. The evaluation behind this is section 11.

  1. WebZjs's NU7 release, with threads. It needs cross-origin isolation for the whole app (gap 4).
  2. The same release without threads. It needs zcash/librustzcash#3270 fixed upstream, because its scan hangs with threads off.
  3. Our own scanner on zcash_client_backend 0.25 with our own encrypted store, on the crate line the harness measures. Its store would have to build subtrees in parallel, or it keeps the harness's one-thread tree.

The application names two routes for the scanner: the official red·bridge release, and a pull request to the upstream store. Paths 1 and 2 both take the first route. The second route is not one of our paths: that pull request sits on older release candidates (section 11.2). Path 3 is in neither route, so it changes the filed text and goes to ZCG. In every path the encrypted store is ours. None of the three is sized.

The scanner and the builder have to be bridged. Read Every protocol crate in WebZjs #27 is at the same version as in Konclave's signer (Table 16), so a PCZT, a viewing key and note data move between them without conversion. What Milestone 2 needs is a data contract: what the scanner hands the builder (notes, their witnesses, the anchor height) and what the builder hands the signer (a PCZT, as today). Read Today's builder does not take that contract as data. konclave-signer calls propose_transfer and create_pczt_from_proposal, which read a wallet store through its traits, today a SQLite store on the coordinator that the web app does not have. Read Two WebAssembly modules built with different wasm-bindgen versions talk only through JavaScript, so Konclave's builder cannot call those traits on a store inside WebZjs's module.

So the bridge takes one of two shapes, and Milestone 2 chooses before it starts. Either the store and the builder link into one module (path 3, or WebZjs's crates built into Konclave's module), or the scanner's notes and witnesses are copied across as data into a store Konclave's module owns. The design keeps propose_transfer either way, so note selection and change are not written a second time. The contract's types would live once, in konclave-seal.

5.8 A vault of phones only

A vault whose members have no computer would scan on a phone while the app is open, show the time of its last sync, and build and prove on that phone. It stays in the new mode, as the slow case. The design does not say which phone scans in a vault of several phones.

The design allows it, with these limits. It scans only while the app is open and visible, since a browser can suspend a hidden tab. The 2-action proof fits a phone of the A72's class and not one of the A14's. No phone met the 30 s one-day bar without threads. With threads and a parallel tree the S24 came to the bar and not under it on live rows, and the A72 stayed far over (section 4.4). Threads need the isolation of gap 4. A wallet scan on a phone was not timed against the bars, and a scan with the app hidden was not measured. An 8-output proof took 223.77 s on the A72 (R50), and no rule yet says whether a payroll on such a vault is split, capped or left slow (gap 7).

A phone could also prove a transaction that a computer built. Milestone 2 could measure that. It is not the default, because building needs the notes and their witnesses, and those live in the scanning computer's wallet.

5.9 Flows that are not designed

  • Handing the books to another computer. How the vault names the books computer, how the role moves when that computer is lost, and how two computers that both build are kept apart.
  • Restoring a device. How a restored device obtains the seats' certificates it seals to, and restoring a vault from its export alone (gap 8).

Each has a row in section 9. The migration of an existing vault is in section 8.2.

  • Giving the viewing key only to the devices that scan. We did not weigh it in detail. A device without the key could not derive the vault's own addresses for its check of a payment (section 5.5), could not check the books, and its backup could not restore the vault.

  • Signing the summary with a seat's share. ADR-0012 D8 rules it out.
  • A suggestion by hanh in the grant's forum topic[22]: move the coordinator's role in the signing ceremony to one of the peers, or have the peers talk to each other directly. Our reply[2]: in the web app that role already runs on a member's device. Rounds by QR or copy-paste would take the relay out of the path. They are planned, not built, and not part of this grant. Peer to peer was not on our roadmap then (13 October 2026). This design keeps the relay.

What the new mode is meant to hold, as properties. Each is proposed, and none is tested.

  • The coordinator cannot obtain a new vault's nk, rivk_ext, sk or qsk.
  • No single member, the one who keeps the books included, can change who is paid. This depends on the two unwritten rules of section 5.5.
  • A device cannot end the creation of a vault holding a viewing key that differs from the other devices': it holds the same key or it refuses. This depends on the commitment to sk (section 5.3).

8.2 The in-place upgrade of a protected vault

  1. Each member's device would fetch the viewing key and the height the scan starts from, check that the key gives the address the device has on record, and keep both sealed. For older records that check is circular: the address and the start height both came from the coordinator (Table 11).
  2. A member's computer would scan with that key until its balance matches the one the coordinator shows. The design does not say how a vault of phones only does this step.
  3. The quorum would sign the request to upgrade, so that one member could not order it. What the quorum signs and who verifies it are open. If the coordinator counts the signatures, a vault created before v0.18.0 carries issue #577, open on 24 November 2026: a member can claim a colleague's seat that its holder has not claimed yet and then vote as them.
  4. The step that cannot be undone. The coordinator would stop answering any request with the viewing key and delete the key and the wallet built from it. It would keep the vault registered in the new mode, with no field for the key. The members cannot check from outside that the deletion was done (section 8.3).
  5. From then on the vault would work as section 5 describes. The funds would not move and the vault's address would not change.
Limit

A vault that reaches step 4 with no device and no backup holding the key can no longer see or spend its own funds. No device could find its notes or build a spend, and the key cannot be generated again.

8.3 What the upgrade cannot undo

The coordinator has held the vault's viewing key, and the key does not change. So whoever ran the coordinator, or obtained a copy of the key, can read the vault's past and future payments for as long as the vault exists. Deleting the coordinator's copies is a procedure. It gives no cryptographic guarantee, and the members cannot verify that no copy remains.

The copy is more than one file. It is the registration record, the wallet's key file and the wallet database built from it. Our operations backups and recovery copies of that storage hold the key, and so does any copy our hosting provider keeps. Every device the coordinator sealed the key to has received it and could have kept a copy. We can delete the ones we control and cannot prove it.

After the upgrade the coordinator would still hold what it holds for a new vault: the members' names, every proposal with its destination, amount and memo, and the record of each payment it built for the vault before the upgrade. Without the key it could no longer see incoming payments or the balance. Milestone 3 would write this down as its statement of what the migration cannot undo[1].

A vault that wants the coordinator's knowledge to stop at a known point would create a new vault in the new mode, once that mode exists, and move its funds with one payment signed by its quorum. The new vault's key would never have been on the coordinator. The old key still reveals everything up to and including that payment.

So the in-place upgrade saves the move of funds and keeps the address. It does not give what a new vault would have: a key the coordinator never held, and an sk (section 9.1).

9 The sk decision, open items and gaps

Where the design has no answer, the row says so. The column "who decides" is our proposal. "With the reviewer's input" means the published review, and asks no further work from the reviewer. An item marked that way is not settled before the review is published (section 14).

9.1 New vaults adopt ZIP 2005

ZIP 2005 has the status Final. Its section "Usage with FROST" constrains key generation for a FROST group: "participants MUST privately agree on a value sk", and use it with use_qsk = true to derive nk, qsk, qk and rivk_ext. In that derivation nk and qsk come from sk, qk comes from qsk, and rivk_ext comes from qk, ak and nk, where ak is the group key the key generation gives (protocol specification, section 4.2.3, as modified by ZIP 2005). The viewing key is ak, nk and rivk_ext, so with an sk every member can derive it.

Read No vault has an sk today. The Foundation's tool that makes the viewing key, zcash-sign generate, draws a random sk, derives nk and rivk from it by the older derivation (use_qsk = false, in which rivk comes from sk alone) and keeps none of it. The application describes the same for new vaults, on a member's device: the viewing key created there and sealed to the others.

Read ZIP 312 as published leaves key generation out of scope. Its draft text, the open pull request zcash/zips#895 at commit 4a58cbd, adds it. It says that to agree on sk "one of the following options SHOULD be used". The first: "Participants generate a sk in a contributory manner", an approach that "SHOULD be used to generate sk if Distributed Key Generation was employed". The second: "One participant generates a random sk and sends it to the others via an encrypted and authenticated channel", an option that "is NOT RECOMMENDED if Distributed Key Generation was employed". Konclave creates every vault by distributed key generation. The implementation check that Daira-Emma Hopwood posted on that pull request on 10 November 2026 says, as its discrepancy 3, that zcash-sign generate "creates sk on one machine and does not share it, which is the option the ZIP marks NOT RECOMMENDED with DKG". What the application describes falls short even of that option as the draft writes it: the draft's second option sends sk to the others over an authenticated channel, and the application's text keeps no sk and sends the derived key over a sealing that does not say who sealed.

Read A draft ZIP for a FROST member backup format is discussed in issue zcash/zips#1413, and its text is pull request zcash/zips#1432, which holds version 1. In the issue, on 23 November 2026, nikkolasg proposed a sentence for version 2, and on 24 November KHADEVS, one of the draft's authors, agreed to add it: key source 0x02 is for groups that already exist without an sk, and a wallet creating a new group SHOULD give it one. Version 2 is not written as ZIP text yet, so this is a commitment and not yet a rule. Version 1 also changed: at commit 40c89aa of the pull request, restore step 2 rejects a signing share of zero, with a test vector in zcash/zcash-test-vectors#136 (commit 79c2c0e).

Read A second open pull request builds on that draft: zcash/zips#1459, by nikkolasg, read at commit 67ab0d2 on 24 November 2026, a set of suggested changes to ZIP 312 on top of zcash/zips#895. Two of them bear most directly on this design.

  • The key generation must run over the same group and base point as the FROST ciphersuite that uses its output. For the Pallas ciphersuite that base point is GOrchard. Otherwise the group key is not a valid ak for the shares.
  • What a participant backs up, and what a restore checks. A participant should back up its signing share and sk directly. With the transcript of the key generation at hand, a restore must check the share against its verifying share computed from the transcript, and Hcommit(sk) against the transcript's extension, which holds the commitment to sk. Without it, sk can be checked against the nk and rivk_ext of the group's full viewing key, and a wrong share shows at the first signature. Since qsk follows from sk, such a backup must be protected at least as securely as qsk, and if it is encrypted, the encryption should not rest only on discrete logarithms.

Like the draft it builds on, zcash/zips#1459 is guidance for a new vault, and both are open. The commitment to sk in the creation record, the export and the restore of gap 8 (Table 12) would follow them. The last point agrees with issue #643 (below).

Read The crates we pin do not expose the use_qsk derivation: orchard 0.16.0 and zcash_spec do not have it. Konclave has its own, derive_use_qsk in konclave-seal (src/zip2005.rs, pull request #1051, merged 17 November 2026). It is checked in CI against vectors produced elsewhere. Its own header states the limit: it uses the same crates as the code that produced the vectors, so a mistake those crates share would pass. No product path calls it, and nobody outside DeegaLabs has reviewed it. Milestone 2 would put it on the creation path.

Our decision of 24 November 2026: new vaults adopt ZIP 2005 in Milestone 2. The members of a new vault agree on an sk, and every device derives nk and rivk_ext from it with use_qsk = true. No device makes the viewing key and seals it to the others, and the export carries sk. A new vault with no sk, which is what every vault is today, is no longer proposed.

A new vault conforms to ZIP 2005, which is Final. The draft text of ZIP 312 is guidance: we follow it where Konclave's key generation allows, and a new vault is not bound to it.

With sk made inside the key generation, the messages that carry its parts are where a party that records the relay could later learn sk. 9.4 The path to the official key-generation standard

The application says: "Since our design creates that material directly on the user's device, we will already indicate the path for the vaults to migrate to the official standard as soon as it is formalized." It also says: "We do not claim to follow a finished standard for key generation."

Read ZIP 312, read on 24 November 2026, has the status Draft and says "key generation is out of scope for this ZIP and the FROST spec". An open draft pull request by Conrado Gouvêa, zcash/zips#895 ("[ZIP 312] Specify key generation, change randomizer handling", open and marked draft on 24 November 2026), would add key generation with COCKTAIL-DKG, saying that other distributed key generation protocols MAY be used, and a contributory generation of sk, which each participant derives from what it decrypts during the key generation. Konclave's key generation is frost-core's PedPop and not COCKTAIL-DKG. We have commented on it since 4 November 2026. We make no claim about whether or in which form it will be accepted.

The application recorded a question to Conrado Gouvêa, of the Zcash Foundation, about creating the viewing key on a member's device (topic 57218, post 10). In it we proposed that one member's device create sk and seal it to the others, which is the alternative of section 9.1 and not what we now propose. On 24 November 2026 it had no reply.

So the path is not final. Inference If zcash/zips#895 is accepted as it stands, a new vault's viewing key would come out of the key generation on every member's device, which is what section 9.1 proposes, with a specified method. Konclave would also have to move to COCKTAIL-DKG, or the text would have to cover PedPop, before that method is Konclave's (the choice is in section 9.1). A vault whose viewing key was drawn at random, which is every vault today, cannot be mapped onto it. Its path would be a new vault and one payment signed by its quorum.

11.4 The fee after block 3,590,000

Read The WebZjs wallet builds every proposal with StandardFeeRule::Zip317 (crates/webzjs-wallet/src/wallet.rs:331, :520, :657, :758 at 9ad0a589). In zcash_client_backend 0.25.0 that rule has a marginal fee of 5,000 zatoshis, with no height switch (zcash_primitives 0.31.0, src/transaction/fees/zip317.rs:19). This is the library's standard rule and applies to every wallet that builds with it, until upstream changes it (zcash/librustzcash#3282 and pull request #3283, both open)[27].

So after block 3,590,000 the WebZjs wallet would pay 5,000 per logical action where ZIP 2009 says 1,000. The transaction is valid. It overpays about fivefold, and it can be told apart from wallets that switched, which ZIP 2009 asks wallets to avoid.

Read Separately, the validator pczt_validate of WebZjs's MetaMask Snap, on red-dev main, refuses fees below 5,000 × max(2, actions), so it would refuse a PCZT that pays the ZIP 2009 fee. Pull request #27 lowers that floor to a 1,000 rate, also with no height switch. We reported it as red-dev-inc/WebZjs#32.

For Konclave this matters only if WebZjs builds transactions. Read Konclave's own builder uses FeeRule::non_standard at the marginal fee the vault allows. The first production payment at 1,000 zatoshis per logical action is transaction 7ac245ad, mined at block 3,590,637[28]. Where not every device of a vault has reported that it accepts the lower fee, the coordinator still pays 5,000 per action, so those vaults overpay and stand out the same way (gap 6).
